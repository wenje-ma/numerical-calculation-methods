> **Jacobi Iteration** (Formula (3.12)) <br>Let the coefficient matrix $A$ of the system $Ax=b$ be split as $A=D-L-U$, where $D=\mathrm{diag}\left(a_{11},a_{22},\dots,a_{nn}\right)$; then the Jacobi iteration scheme is $$x^{\left(k+1\right)}=Bx^{\left(k\right)}+g,\quad B=I-D^{-1}A,\quad g=D^{-1}b,$$ whose component form is $$x_i^{\left(k+1\right)}=\frac{1}{a_{ii}}\left(b_i-\sum_{j\ne i}a_{ij}x_j^{\left(k\right)}\right),\ i=1,2,\dots,n.$$

> **Gauss–Seidel Iteration** (Formula (3.15)) <br>In Jacobi iteration, as soon as a new component $x_i^{\left(k+1\right)}$ is computed it is immediately used in the computation of the subsequent components; this gives the Gauss–Seidel iteration, whose matrix form is $$x^{\left(k+1\right)}=\left(D-L\right)^{-1}Ux^{\left(k\right)}+\left(D-L\right)^{-1}b,$$ with iteration matrix $M=\left(D-L\right)^{-1}U$.

> **Relaxation Iteration (Successive Over-Relaxation) ** (Formula (3.17)) <br>Introducing a relaxation factor $\omega$ into the Gauss–Seidel iteration gives $$x^{\left(k+1\right)}=\left(D-\omega L\right)^{-1}\left[\left(1-\omega\right)D+\omega U\right]x^{\left(k\right)}+\omega\left(D-\omega L\right)^{-1}b,$$ the iteration matrix is $M_{\omega}=\left(D-\omega L\right)^{-1}\left[\left(1-\omega\right)D+\omega U\right]$; when $\omega=1$, this is exactly the Gauss–Seidel iteration.

> **Definition 3.3** (Spectral Radius) <br>Let $A$ be a square matrix of order $n$ and let $\lambda_i\left(i=1,2,\dots,n\right)$ be the eigenvalues of $A$. The maximum of the absolute values of the eigenvalues is called the spectral radius of the matrix $A$, denoted $$\rho\left(A\right)=\max_{1\le i\le n}\left|\lambda_i\right|.$$

> **Theorem 3.6** (Necessary and Sufficient Condition for Convergence of Iterative Methods) <br>For any initial vector $x^{\left(0\right)}$ and right-hand side $g$, the vector sequence generated by the iteration $x^{\left(k+1\right)}=Mx^{\left(k\right)}+g$ converges if and only if $$\rho\left(M\right)< 1.$$

> **Definition 3.4** (Diagonally Dominant Matrix) <br>If a square matrix of order $n$ $A=\left(a_{ij}\right)$ satisfies $$\left|a_{ii}\right|\ge\sum_{j\ne i}\left|a_{ij}\right|\quad\left(i=1,2,\dots,n\right),$$ and at least one $i$ satisfies the strict inequality, then $A$ is called a weakly diagonally dominant matrix; if the strict inequality holds for all $i$, then $A$ is called a strictly diagonally dominant matrix.

> **Some Commonly Used Sufficient Convergence Conditions** (Convergence Criteria) <br>For the system $Ax=b$: (1) if $A$ is strictly diagonally dominant (or irreducibly and weakly diagonally dominant), then both Jacobi and Gauss–Seidel iteration converge; (2) if $A$ is symmetric positive-definite and $0< \omega< 2$, then the relaxation method converges (this condition is necessary and sufficient).

### Problem 1 (Problem 3-3)

Use Jacobi iteration and Gauss–Seidel iteration to solve the system

$$
\begin{cases}x_1+2x_2-2x_3=1,\\x_1+x_2+x_3=1,\\2x_1+2x_2+x_3=1,\end{cases}
$$

taking the initial value $x^{\left(0\right)}=\left(0,0,0\right)^{\top}$, iterate 4 times, and compare the computed results.

### Solution to Problem 1

The coefficient matrix is $A=\begin{bmatrix}1&2&-2\\1&1&1\\2&2&1\end{bmatrix}$, $b=\left(1,1,1\right)^{\top}$.

By **Jacobi Iteration** (Formula (3.12)), since $a_{ii}=1$, the iteration formula is

$$
x_1^{\left(k+1\right)}=1-2x_2^{\left(k\right)}+2x_3^{\left(k\right)},
$$

$$
x_2^{\left(k+1\right)}=1-x_1^{\left(k\right)}-x_3^{\left(k\right)},
$$

$$
x_3^{\left(k+1\right)}=1-2x_1^{\left(k\right)}-2x_2^{\left(k\right)}.
$$

Starting from $x^{\left(0\right)}=\left(0,0,0\right)^{\top}$, one obtains

$$
x^{\left(1\right)}=\left(1,1,1\right)^{\top},\quad x^{\left(2\right)}=\left(1,-1,-3\right)^{\top},
$$

$$
x^{\left(3\right)}=\left(-3,3,1\right)^{\top},\quad x^{\left(4\right)}=\left(-3,3,1\right)^{\top}.
$$

By **Gauss–Seidel Iteration** (Formula (3.15)), the iteration formula is

$$
x_1^{\left(k+1\right)}=1-2x_2^{\left(k\right)}+2x_3^{\left(k\right)},
$$

$$
x_2^{\left(k+1\right)}=1-x_1^{\left(k+1\right)}-x_3^{\left(k\right)},
$$

$$
x_3^{\left(k+1\right)}=1-2x_1^{\left(k+1\right)}-2x_2^{\left(k+1\right)}.
$$

Starting from $x^{\left(0\right)}=\left(0,0,0\right)^{\top}$, one obtains

$$
x^{\left(1\right)}=\left(1,0,-1\right)^{\top},\quad x^{\left(2\right)}=\left(-1,3,-3\right)^{\top},
$$

$$
x^{\left(3\right)}=\left(-11,15,-7\right)^{\top},\quad x^{\left(4\right)}=\left(-43,51,-15\right)^{\top}.
$$

Comparison: Jacobi iteration reaches the exact solution $x=\left(-3,3,1\right)^{\top}$ already at step 3 (substituting into the original system verifies $Ax=\left(1,1,1\right)^{\top}=b$), and stays unchanged at step 4; the absolute values of the Gauss–Seidel components grow rapidly, showing a divergent trend. In fact, $D=I$, and the Jacobi iteration matrix $B=I-A$ satisfies the characteristic equation $\det\left(\lambda I-B\right)=\lambda^{3}=0$, so $\rho\left(B\right)=0< 1$; whereas the characteristic equation of the Gauss–Seidel iteration matrix $M=\left(D-L\right)^{-1}U$ is $\det\left(\lambda I-M\right)=\lambda\left(\lambda-2\right)^{2}=0$, with eigenvalues $0,2,2$, so $\rho\left(M\right)=2> 1$. By **Theorem 3.6** (Necessary and Sufficient Condition for Convergence of Iterative Methods), Jacobi iteration converges while Gauss–Seidel iteration diverges. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 2 (Problem 3-5)

If Jacobi iteration is used to solve the system

$$
\begin{bmatrix}a&-1&-3\\-1&a&-2\\3&-2&a\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x_1\\x_2\\x_3\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}b_1\\b_2\\b_3\end{bmatrix},
$$

discuss how the real number $a$ is related to convergence.

### Solution to Problem 2

By **Jacobi Iteration** (Formula (3.12)), $D=\mathrm{diag}\left(a,a,a\right)$, and the Jacobi iteration matrix is

$$
B=I-D^{-1}A=\begin{bmatrix}0&1/a&3/a\\1/a&0&2/a\\-3/a&2/a&0\end{bmatrix}=\frac{1}{a}C,\quad C=\begin{bmatrix}0&1&3\\1&0&2\\-3&2&0\end{bmatrix}.
$$

The characteristic equation of $C$ is

$$
\det\left(\lambda I-C\right)=\det\begin{bmatrix}\lambda&-1&-3\\-1&\lambda&-2\\3&-2&\lambda\end{bmatrix}=\lambda^{3}+4\lambda=\lambda\left(\lambda^{2}+4\right)=0,
$$

so the eigenvalues of $C$ are $0,\ 2i,\ -2i$, and hence the eigenvalues of $B$ are $0,\ \frac{2i}{a},\ -\frac{2i}{a}$. By **Definition 3.3** (Spectral Radius), $$
\rho\left(B\right)=\left|\frac{2}{a}\right|.
$$

By **Theorem 3.6** (Necessary and Sufficient Condition for Convergence of Iterative Methods), Jacobi iteration converges if and only if $\rho\left(B\right)< 1$, i.e. $\left|\frac{2}{a}\right|< 1$, which is to say

$$
\left|a\right|> 2\quad\left(a\ne0\right).
$$

Thus Jacobi iteration converges if and only if the real number $a$ satisfies $\left|a\right|> 2$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 3 (Problem 3-7)

Consider the system

$$
\begin{cases}10x_1+4x_2+4x_3=13,\\4x_1+10x_2+8x_3=11,\\4x_1+8x_2+10x_3=25.\end{cases}
$$ (1) Write out the iteration formulas and iteration matrices for Jacobi iteration, Gauss–Seidel iteration, and successive over-relaxation ($\omega=1.35$); (2) for any initial value, does each iterative method converge? Give reasons.

### Solution to Problem 3

The coefficient matrix is $A=\begin{bmatrix}10&4&4\\4&10&8\\4&8&10\end{bmatrix}$, $b=\left(13,11,25\right)^{\top}$, $D=\mathrm{diag}\left(10,10,10\right)$. (1) By **Jacobi Iteration** (Formula (3.12)), the iteration formula is

$$
x_1^{\left(k+1\right)}=1.3-0.4x_2^{\left(k\right)}-0.4x_3^{\left(k\right)},
$$

$$
x_2^{\left(k+1\right)}=1.1-0.4x_1^{\left(k\right)}-0.8x_3^{\left(k\right)},
$$

$$
x_3^{\left(k+1\right)}=2.5-0.4x_1^{\left(k\right)}-0.8x_2^{\left(k\right)},
$$

the iteration matrix is

$$
B=\begin{bmatrix}0&-0.4&-0.4\\-0.4&0&-0.8\\-0.4&-0.8&0\end{bmatrix}.
$$

By **Gauss–Seidel Iteration** (Formula (3.15)), the iteration formula is

$$
x_1^{\left(k+1\right)}=1.3-0.4x_2^{\left(k\right)}-0.4x_3^{\left(k\right)},
$$

$$
x_2^{\left(k+1\right)}=1.1-0.4x_1^{\left(k+1\right)}-0.8x_3^{\left(k\right)},
$$

$$
x_3^{\left(k+1\right)}=2.5-0.4x_1^{\left(k+1\right)}-0.8x_2^{\left(k+1\right)},
$$

with iteration matrix $M=\left(D-L\right)^{-1}U$.

By **Relaxation Iteration (Successive Over-Relaxation) ** (Formula (3.17)), with $\omega=1.35$, the iteration formula is

$$
x_1^{\left(k+1\right)}=-0.35x_1^{\left(k\right)}+1.35\left(1.3-0.4x_2^{\left(k\right)}-0.4x_3^{\left(k\right)}\right),
$$

$$
x_2^{\left(k+1\right)}=-0.35x_2^{\left(k\right)}+1.35\left(1.1-0.4x_1^{\left(k+1\right)}-0.8x_3^{\left(k\right)}\right),
$$

$$
x_3^{\left(k+1\right)}=-0.35x_3^{\left(k\right)}+1.35\left(2.5-0.4x_1^{\left(k+1\right)}-0.8x_2^{\left(k+1\right)}\right),
$$

with iteration matrix $M_{\omega}=\left(D-\omega L\right)^{-1}\left[\left(1-\omega\right)D+\omega U\right]$. (2) First check the positive definiteness of $A$: $A^{\top}=A$ is symmetric, and its leading principal minors are

$$
\Delta_1=10> 0,\quad\Delta_2=\det\begin{bmatrix}10&4\\4&10\end{bmatrix}=84> 0,
$$

$$
\Delta_3=\det A=10\left(100-64\right)-4\left(40-32\right)+4\left(32-40\right)=296> 0,
$$

so $A$ is a symmetric positive-definite matrix. By (2) of **Some Commonly Used Sufficient Convergence Conditions** (Convergence Criteria), since $0< \omega=1.35< 2$, successive over-relaxation converges; Gauss–Seidel iteration corresponds to $\omega=1$ and also converges.

For Jacobi iteration, the characteristic equation of $B$ is

$$
\det\left(\lambda I-B\right)=\det\begin{bmatrix}\lambda&0.4&0.4\\0.4&\lambda&0.8\\0.4&0.8&\lambda\end{bmatrix}=\lambda^{3}-0.96\lambda+0.256=0.
$$

Factoring gives $\left(\lambda-0.8\right)\left(\lambda^{2}+0.8\lambda-0.32\right)=0$; the three eigenvalues are $0.8,\ 0.293,\ -1.093$, so $\rho\left(B\right)\approx1.093> 1$. By **Theorem 3.6** (Necessary and Sufficient Condition for Convergence of Iterative Methods), Jacobi iteration diverges.

In summary, for any initial value, Gauss–Seidel iteration and successive over-relaxation ($\omega=1.35$) converge, while Jacobi iteration diverges. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 4 (Problem 3-8)

Consider the system

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1.2&-3.6&-12\\-10&9&0.5\\1&-4&2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x_1\\x_2\\x_3\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1\\2\\3\end{bmatrix},
$$

write out a convergent iteration scheme and explain why it converges.

### Solution to Problem 4

The original coefficient matrix $A=\begin{bmatrix}1.2&-3.6&-12\\-10&9&0.5\\1&-4&2\end{bmatrix}$ is not diagonally dominant (first row $\left|1.2\right|< 3.6+12$, second row $\left|9\right|< 10+0.5$, third row $\left|2\right|< 1+4$). Swap the order of the equations (i.e. perform row swaps on the coefficient matrix, which does not change the solution); taking the new equation order as the original second, third, and first rows gives the equivalent system

$$
\begin{bmatrix}-10&9&0.5\\1&-4&2\\1.2&-3.6&-12\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x_1\\x_2\\x_3\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}2\\3\\1\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Check diagonal dominance row by row: $$
\left|-10\right|=10> 9+0.5=9.5,\quad\left|-4\right|=4> 1+2=3,\quad\left|-12\right|=12> 1.2+3.6=4.8.
$$

By **Definition 3.4** (Diagonally Dominant Matrix), the rearranged coefficient matrix is a **strictly diagonally dominant matrix**. By (1) of **Some Commonly Used Sufficient Convergence Conditions** (Convergence Criteria), both Jacobi iteration and Gauss–Seidel iteration converge.

Write out the convergent Jacobi iteration scheme (for the rearranged system): $$
x_1^{\left(k+1\right)}=\frac{2-9x_2^{\left(k\right)}-0.5x_3^{\left(k\right)}}{-10}=-0.2+0.9x_2^{\left(k\right)}+0.05x_3^{\left(k\right)},
$$

$$
x_2^{\left(k+1\right)}=\frac{3-x_1^{\left(k\right)}-2x_3^{\left(k\right)}}{-4}=-0.75+0.25x_1^{\left(k\right)}+0.5x_3^{\left(k\right)},
$$

$$
x_3^{\left(k+1\right)}=\frac{1-1.2x_1^{\left(k\right)}+3.6x_2^{\left(k\right)}}{-12}=-\frac{1}{12}+0.1x_1^{\left(k\right)}-0.3x_2^{\left(k\right)}.
$$

By **Theorem 3.6** (Necessary and Sufficient Condition for Convergence of Iterative Methods), this iteration scheme converges for any initial value. $\blacksquare$
